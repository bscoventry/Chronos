Chronos: An open-source, closed loop neuromodulation platform.

Welcome to the home of the Chronos project!

![description](https://github.com/bscoventry/Chronos/image.png)

There is a crucial disparity between the evaluation of neuromodulation therapies in preclinical animla models and their clinical application. 
While chronic stimulation in humans produces long-term neural adaptation, entrainment phenomena, and biomolecular remodeling, most preclinical 
studies rely on short-term, acute stimulation paradigms that fail to capture these processes. Consequently, important aspects of therapeutic 
efficacy, durability, and adverse effect profiles remain poorly understood prior to translation to human studies. This disparity limits the 
predictive power of preclinical experiments and contributes to the high attrition rate of new neuromodulation targets and strategies during 
clinical deployment. 

Implantable pulse generators (IPGs) for human use are cost-prohibitive for conducting large animal experiments with the necessary statistical 
power to reveal anything but the most marked of differences. Similarly, clinical IPGs are too large for implantation in high throughput small 
animal models such as rodents. To address this need, a number of preclinical IPG platforms have been proposed. Existing preclinical platforms 
have primarily relied on application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs)1–5 which, although power-efficient and amenable to miniaturization, 
suffer from low fabrication yields, limited design iteration, and restricted post-fabrication flexibility. These systems typically require 
specialized fabrication facilities, custom test infrastructure, and significant upfront development effort, limiting their accessibility to a 
small number of highly specialized laboratories. Furthermore, ASIC-based designs are often optimized for a narrow set of stimulation parameters, 
making it difficult to adapt them to evolving experimental needs or emerging neuromodulation paradigms.

Here we present the Chronos IPG to meet these needs. Chronos is a chronic stimulation platform that also offers sensing capabilities and closed-
loop control all powered on an implanted battery. Communication with Chronos is achieved through a low power blue tooth link and GUI software
capable of running on most computer systems or small micro computers (such as a Raspberry Pi).
